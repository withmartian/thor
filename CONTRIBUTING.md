# <img src="images/thor-mark.png" width="40" alt=""> Contributing to thor

thor is a small, curated framework rather than a large open codebase, so it is
maintained by hand rather than through open pull requests. The most valuable
contribution is simple: **tell me what happened when you used it** — especially
what broke, what felt over-strict, and what you had to change to make it fit your
work.

## How to propose an improvement

Please get in touch directly rather than opening a pull request:

- **Contact:** Philip Quirke (Martian) — <philip@withmartian.com>

When you reach out, it helps to include:

1. **What you were doing** — the kind of project, roughly how long, how many
   threads, which models/vendors.
2. **Which templates you used** — the whole set, or a subset (many projects use
   only `thor-document-rules.md` and `thor-glossary.md` to start).
3. **The specific friction or idea** — the template or practice involved, what
   went wrong or what you'd change, and, if you can, the wording you'd suggest.

Concrete before/after Markdown is ideal, but a plain description of the problem
is just as useful — I would rather hear the problem than nothing.

## What tends to make a good change

thor earns its keep by staying small and honest. Changes are most likely to land
when they:

- **remove** a step that is not pulling its weight, or make one cheaper, rather
  than adding process for its own sake;
- keep the **empirical / speculative separation** intact (results docs never
  argue conjecture; conjecture docs never smuggle in results);
- keep each document to **one clear job and one clear owner**;
- come with a real example of the friction they fix.

## Reusing the templates

You do not need permission to use, copy, or adapt the templates — that is what
they are for, under the [MIT license](LICENSE). Contributing back is a bonus, not
an obligation. Adapt freely; the templates serve the work, not the other way
around.
